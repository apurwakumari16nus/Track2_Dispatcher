The problem

iPhone
SweaterCo makes garments through eight outside workshops. One dispatcher decides where every order goes, from a busy group chat, with dates, cost and quality pulling against each other. Get it wrong and orders come back late. Always picking the cheapest workshop makes 92 percent of batches late, and even choosing at random makes 34 percent late. Threadline is for that dispatcher, for workshops who already live in Telegram, and for clients who want to hear about a delay first. It is a native iPhone app joined to Telegram, built on one rule: the model reads, Python decides, a person approves.

Evidence

The evidence. In the course simulator, 120 orders over ten seeds, 1.7 percent of batches were late, and 1.9 with a workshop closed. 4:01

Limitations

iPhone
Now where it breaks. On lateness we only tie a simple earliest-finish rule. Our gain is the checks, reasons and recovery. Speed costs quality: 7 percent of pieces defective against 3 at best, and one workshop takes 26 percent of the work. A question about delivery got a clarifying question instead of a refusal, and a request to split was read as one order. A whole-number price in Chinese was dropped, so the desk asks again. Workshops are role-played, and one Mac runs the desk. Next we would host it and test with real workshops.

2 minutes liveLikely questions

  • Are workshops real? No. They are role-played in real Telegram, which the professor allowed. The reading is live.
  • What does the model decide? Nothing. It reads. Allocation, dates and plans are Python, and a person approves.
  • Why not just use earliest finish? On lateness you could, and we tie it. The system adds checks, reasons, recovery and the loop with workshops.
  • How do you know the numbers are fair? The simulator is the course's. The 16 chat requests and 12 replies were unseen. The 48 replies were used to fix the reader, and we say so.
  • What fails most? Reading was right on meaning. Failures are specific values, such as a whole-number price in Chinese, and they fail safely by asking again.
  • Why both an app and Telegram? Workshops will not install an app for one customer, but they already answer Telegram. A chat cannot show who has room or what breaks if a workshop closes.
